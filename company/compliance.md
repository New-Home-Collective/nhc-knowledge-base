## Fair Housing Rules

Mandatory. Every brand, every tool, every time.

### Never describe the people

Never reference, imply, or describe the people who live in an area, or who
would live in a home, by any of these:

- Race, color, or national origin
- Religion
- Sex, gender identity, or sexual orientation
- Familial status, which includes "great for families" used on its own
- Disability

Never describe who would or would not fit in a home or a neighborhood. Not in
listing copy, not in a blog post, not in a caption, not in a phone script,
not in a cabin description.

### Describe the property and the place instead

These are safe because they are facts about the thing, not about the people:

- Condition, repairs needed, age, size, lot
- Price, price range, median price
- Location, commute time, walkability
- Number of bedrooms and bathrooms, layout, square footage
- Proximity to schools, parks, restaurants, and shops

Let the data speak.

### The rewrite most people need

Say: "near top-rated schools"
Not: "great school district for your kids"

The first is a fact about where the house is. The second is a guess about who
is buying it.

## Where this applies

**New Home Collective.** All listing copy, blog posts, social posts, bios,
seller and buyer materials, and anything Sadie says on the phone.

**BE Property Ventures.** All investor, cash offer, seller, and wholesale
content. Buying a house does not exempt the advertising around it.

**Lake Days, Cabin Stays.** Short-term rental listings and travel content are
covered too. The Fair Housing Act reaches advertising for dwellings, and a
cabin someone stays in is a dwelling. Describe the cabin, the lake, the
drive, and the amenities. Do not describe who the cabin is for.
