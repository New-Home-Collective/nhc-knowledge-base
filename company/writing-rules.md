# Universal Writing Rules

Last updated: September 14, 2026
Last verified: September 14, 2026
Owner: Bob Sophiea. Maintainer: Madison Feldman.
Review cycle: quarterly.

This file owns the writing rules for every brand. New Home Collective,
BE Property Ventures, and Lake Days, Cabin Stays all write this way.

Nothing else may hold its own copy. If you find a second copy anywhere,
delete it and point at this file instead.

If you cannot read this file, say so and stop. Do not write from memory.

---

## The rules

These apply to every word we publish. No exceptions.

- **No em dashes.** Use a period or rewrite the sentence.
- **Fifth grade reading level.** Short sentences. Common words.
- **Short paragraphs.** Two to four sentences max. One idea per paragraph.
- **Punchy line breaks.** Let lines breathe.
- **Contractions.** Use them naturally.
- **Conversational.** Write how Bob talks, not how a lawyer writes.
- **Storytelling over polish.** A real moment beats a perfect sentence.
- **Single-word lines for emphasis** when the moment calls for it.
- **Active voice.** Always.
- **"You" and "your."** Speak directly to one person, not an audience.
- **No jargon** without a plain-language explanation.
- **Reading level check.** If a 10-year-old would struggle, rewrite it.

---

## Jargon, by brand

The rule above is the same everywhere. What counts as jargon is not.

**BE Property Ventures.** Most sellers do not know what ARV, assignment, or
subject-to means. Explain it or drop it.

**New Home Collective.** Assume nothing about contracts, contingencies,
escrow, or appraisal gaps. Say what the word means in the same sentence.

**Lake Days, Cabin Stays.** Booking and rental terms are not common
knowledge either. Same treatment.

## Open item

This file merges two rule sets that had drifted apart, one in the New Home
Collective brand file and one in the BE brand file. Nothing was invented and
nothing was dropped.

Three differences were resolved:

- BE wrote "Fifth grade," NHC wrote "Fifth-grade." Kept BE's, no hyphen.
- NHC had "one idea per paragraph" and a reading level check. BE did not.
  Both kept, since neither contradicts anything.
- On speaking to the reader, NHC said "directly to the reader" and BE said
  "to one person, not an audience." Combined, because they were reaching for
  the same idea.

BE's ARV and assignment note moved to the jargon section, since it is a BE
fact, not a universal rule.
