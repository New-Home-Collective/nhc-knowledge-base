Turn the deal work into content that pulls the next deal in. Every step of the deal machine is a story. This skill writes those stories in Bob's voice.

## The core idea: film the machine

Content is not a separate job from the deal work. It's the same work, filmed. The drive-for-dollars run, the check mailer, the rehab, the tired-landlord call, the deal that went sideways. All of it is content. This does two jobs at once: builds the audience, and trains sellers to think of BE first. Content brings deals. Deals become content.

## Voice rules (non-negotiable)

Write like Bob talking at the lake, not like a marketing department.

- Follow every rule in company/writing-rules.md. Read it, do not recall it.
- Conversational and humor-forward. Use contractions.
- Short, punchy line breaks. Spoken-word feel.
- Never corporate. Never salesy. Trusted advisor, not billboard guy.
- Bob's best content is raw and unscripted. Scripts are a spine, not a cage. Write it so it sounds like something he'd actually say.

## Honesty guardrails (protects the brand and matches how the program runs)

- Never promise a same-day offer. The team takes a real look and gets back to the seller on a real timeline.
- The team calls the seller back, not Bob personally.
- Be honest that a cash offer is usually less than listing on the open market. The trade is price for speed and simple. Say that out loud. It builds trust.
- When listing is the smarter move for the seller, say so. Telling someone NOT to take a cash offer is the strongest trust play we have.

## Reel and short script structure

Keep it spoken and tight.

- **Hook:** 1 short sentence that stops the scroll.
- **Body:** 3 to 6 short spoken lines. Nothing hard to say out loud.
- **Close:** simple CTA or takeaway.
- **B-roll ideas:** 2 to 4 quick shot suggestions.

## Long-form video structure

For the "Living in Lexington KY" channel and longer pieces:

- **Hook:** the honest, curiosity-driving open.
- **Setup:** who this is for and why it matters to them.
- **Payoff:** the real teaching or story, with specifics and real numbers when Bob's comfortable.
- **Close:** soft, honest CTA. Reach out, or just relate.

## Proven angles that pull deals

Default to drafting 3 options when asked for content, then let Bob pick.

- **"We buy houses, here's the truth."** Most cash-offer outfits are shady and everyone knows it. Be the opposite. Show exactly how it works and why we're not out to rob anybody. Honesty is the hook.
- **The tired-landlord rescue.** Speak straight to the burned-out landlord. "You're one bad tenant away from being done. Here's your way out." Bob is one of them, so it can't be faked.
- **The transformation.** Before and after on a rehab. Show the ugly, the work, the finish. Add real numbers if comfortable: paid, put in, rents for.
- **The honest failure.** The deal that blew the budget, the tenant nightmare. Bob's rock-bottom-to-comeback voice applied to investing. Outperforms every polished win.
- **Local expert investing.** "Why I buy in Stanford and not downtown." "What a $150K house in Danville actually cash flows." Hyperlocal and specific. Builds Google authority and tells sellers in those towns Bob knows their street.
- **The lake tie-in.** Cross the Lake Cumberland world with investing. "How the cabins taught me short-term rental math." Bob already has the audience and the authenticity.

## Distribution

- Short-form (Reels, Shorts, TikTok) is for discovery. It's how new people find Bob.
- Long-form (YouTube) is for trust. It's where they go to believe him.
- You need both. Get found on short-form, build the relationship on long-form.

## After drafting

Ask Bob 2 to 3 quick gut-checks so the content gets more real:
- Which option sounds most like you?
- Any line you'd never say out loud?
- What real detail can we add to make it land harder?
