## Step 1: Gather Agent Information

If the agent hasn't provided their info, ask for it using this intake list.
Work with what you have, but more detail produces better output.

**Core Info (required)**
- Full name
- How long they've been in real estate
- City/area specialty within Lexington or Central KY (if any)
- Primary client type: buyers, sellers, both, investors, first-time buyers, relocations?
- One thing they do differently or better than most agents

**Proof Points (get at least 1-2)**
- Homes closed or career transaction volume (approx. is fine)
- Years living in Lexington or Central KY
- Specific neighborhoods they know best
- Any designations, certifications, or training
- Google reviews or Zillow reviews (count or rating)

**Personal Story (what makes them human)**
- What brought them to real estate
- What they love about Lexington or their community
- Something personal — family, hobbies, local roots, or a defining experience
- How they describe their communication style

**Platform(s) needed**
- Website bio, Zillow, Google Business Profile, Instagram, LinkedIn, Facebook, or all?

## Step 2: Identify the Bio Strategy

**For agents who focus on buyers:**
Lead with: finding the right home, navigating the process, negotiating hard.
Proof of expertise beats years of experience. What did you actually DO for people?
First-time buyer specialists: emphasize education, patience, hand-holding.

**For agents who focus on sellers:**
Lead with: reputation, local market knowledge, marketing system.
Sellers choose agents based on reputation first, trust second.
Price it right. Sell it fast. That's what they hired you for.

**For newer agents:**
Don't pretend to have production you don't have. Lead with what IS real:
- Local roots (how long they've lived here)
- Full team support (NHC, Lexington's most-reviewed team)
- Availability and responsiveness
- Personal commitment to learning and client service

**For experienced agents:**
Lead with production, then personality. Numbers earn attention. Story earns trust.

## Step 3: Write the Bio

### Full Website Bio (200-400 words)

**Paragraph 1 — The Hook (2-3 sentences)**
Who they are, who they help, and where they work. Include: full name,
Lexington KY, NHC. No generic openers. Start with something specific and real.

**Paragraph 2 — Local Credibility + Proof (3-4 sentences)**
How long in Lexington. Neighborhoods or areas they know. One concrete proof
point (closings, reviews, years, specialty). What they actually do for
clients that others don't.

**Paragraph 3 — What It's Like to Work With Them (2-3 sentences)**
Communication style. How they show up for clients. The process from their
point of view. Responsiveness, honesty, and process knowledge should come
through in their voice, not as a checklist.

**Paragraph 4 — The Human Part (2-3 sentences)**
Personal. Local. Real. Family, community, hobbies, what they love about
Lexington. This is what makes people like them before they've met.

**Closing Line — Soft CTA**
One sentence. How to reach them. Natural, not salesy.

### Short Bio Versions (produce these after the full bio, without being asked)

**Zillow / Realtor.com (150-200 words)**
Compress the full bio. Keep: name, specialty, proof point, local credibility,
communication style, CTA. Cut: personal story details, excess adjectives.

**Google Business Profile (under 750 characters)**
Lead with keywords: [Name] is a [specialty] real estate agent at New Home
Collective in Lexington, KY. Include: years or closings, neighborhoods,
contact prompt.

**Instagram Bio (under 150 characters)**
Format: Who you help | Local cred | How to reach you
Example: Helping Lexington buyers find home | 7 yrs local | NHC team | [office phone]

Do not type the office phone number in this file or the skill file. Read it
from brands/nhc/BRAND-VOICE.md, under Identity Facts, every time. If that
file cannot be reached, say so and stop. A wrong office number has already
shipped to the public once.

**One-liner (for listings, email sigs, team pages)**
1 sentence. Name + specialty + team + location.
Example: Sarah Thompson is a buyer's agent with New Home Collective,
specializing in first-time buyers across Lexington and surrounding Central
Kentucky communities.

## Step 4: SEO and AI Optimization Check

**Google SEO**
- Passes every rule in company/writing-rules.md (read it, do not recall it)
- Full name in first sentence
- "Lexington, KY" or "Lexington, Kentucky" appears in first paragraph
- "New Home Collective" appears at least once (website bio)
- At least one of: specialty, client type, neighborhood mentioned
- No keyword stuffing, reads naturally
- No generic filler phrases

**AI Search (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google AI Overview)**
- An AI can answer "Who is [agent name]?" from this bio
- An AI can answer "What does [agent name] specialize in?"
- An AI can answer "Is [agent name] affiliated with a team or brokerage?"
- Specific enough that it doesn't sound like every other agent bio

**Conversion**
- Reputation signal present (reviews, team affiliation, or production)
- Responsiveness mentioned or implied
- Local knowledge evident (specific, not generic)
- Trust language present (honest, straightforward, no hype)
- Clear what to do next (CTA or contact)

**NHC Brand**
- Conversational, not corporate
- Short sentences and natural contractions
- Sounds like a real person, not a marketing department

## Step 5: Deliver and Offer Next Steps

Present the bios clearly labeled by platform. Then offer:
1. "Want me to adjust the tone, more formal, more casual, more personal?"
2. "I can add specific neighborhood names or local landmarks if you tell me their market area."
3. "If you have Google reviews or a production number to include, I can weave that in."
4. "Want a version formatted for your listing presentation or recruiting materials?"

---

## Quality Standards

A bio fails if it:
- Could have been written for any agent in any city
- Uses the phrase "passionate about real estate" without irony
- Doesn't include the agent's name in the first sentence
- Mentions Lexington zero times in the website version
- Reads like it was written by a committee

A bio succeeds if:
- A stranger reads it and immediately knows who this person helps and why to call them
- Google can index it and serve it for "[agent name] Lexington KY real estate"
- A client tells the agent "I felt like I knew you before we even talked"
- It doesn't sound like any other agent's bio on the NHC team
