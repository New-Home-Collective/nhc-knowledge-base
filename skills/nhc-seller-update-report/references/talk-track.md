# Talk Track

Produce this in the chat after the report is built. Not as a file. Agents read it on their phone
before they walk in.

Keep it to roughly one screen. Match it to the classification (ON TRACK, EARLY SIGNAL, STALLED).

## The shape of the meeting

The report is sequenced to do the work. The agent's job is mostly to stop talking at the right
moments.

**1. Open with the win. (30 seconds)**
"We got nineteen showings in about three months. That means the photos worked and buyers wanted to
see it."
Nobody gets defensive at good news. This buys you the rest of the meeting.

**2. Hand them page 1 and stop talking.**
This is the most important instruction in the whole track. Let them read the feedback wall
themselves. Do not narrate it. Do not point at quotes. The silence is uncomfortable and it is
supposed to be. They will get there on their own, and when they do, it is their conclusion instead
of yours.

**3. Ask one question when they look up.**
"What are you seeing?"
Then be quiet again. Most sellers will name the pattern out loud. That is the entire goal of the
meeting. If they name it, you have already won.

**4. Walk page 2.**
Now the numbers. Comps first, then the structural explanation. Frame it as why the market reads it
differently, never as their mistake. If there is direct competition that just listed, save it for
last on this page. It lands hardest.

**5. Walk page 3.**
The traffic chart, then the options. Read the options neutrally. Do not lean. Do not say "I'd
recommend." The moment an agent picks for them, it becomes the agent's idea and they will resist
it.

**6. Close with the question.**
"Which matters more to you right now, the price or the timeline?"
Then stop. Their answer tells you which path they are on, and the rest of the conversation writes
itself.

## The three objections that always come

**"Maybe we just need better marketing."**
Point at the showing count. Nineteen showings is not a marketing problem. Marketing gets people
through the door and that already happened. What we are solving is what happens after they walk in.

**"Can't we just wait for the right buyer?"**
Show the neighborhood pace. If homes there are going under contract in under two weeks and this one
is at eighty-nine days, the right buyers are coming through, they are just choosing something else.
Waiting is a real option, and it has a real cost. Name both.

**"We already dropped the price."**
Show the traffic chart. The reduction bought two weeks of attention and then it went quiet. That is
what happens when a cut gets noticed but does not clear the objection. Be direct here without being
harsh: a small trim does not fix a permanent condition.

## Tone

Trusted advisor, never salesperson. The agent is not there to get a price reduction. The agent is
there to make sure the seller can make an informed decision.

If the seller decides to hold at the current price, that is a legitimate outcome. Document it,
agree on a check-in date, and move on. Pressure ruins the relationship and the referral.

## Variations by classification

**ON TRACK** — Skip the objection handling entirely. Ten minutes. Celebrate the win, confirm what
happens next, and ask if anything has changed on their end.

**EARLY SIGNAL** — Softer. Frame the whole thing as getting ahead of it. "I don't want to wait
sixty days to tell you what I'm already seeing." Push the fixable items hard and keep price light.
Set a specific date to revisit.

**STALLED** — The full track above.
